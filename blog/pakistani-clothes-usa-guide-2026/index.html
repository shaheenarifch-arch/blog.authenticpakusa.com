What counts as "authentic" Pakistani clothing?

Authentic Pakistani clothing is defined by its traditional fabrics, such as lawn, cotton, chiffon, khaddar, karandi and silk, and its traditional handwork or machine embroidery, such as chikankari, zardozi, gota and mirror work, cut in classic South Asian silhouettes.

"Authentic" is less about a single label and more about how the garment is made. The shalwar kameez itself has a long history across the region; if you'd like the background, the Wikipedia overview of the shalwar kameez is a good starting point. When you shop, these are the fabrics you'll see most often:

  • Lawn: a fine, lightweight, breathable cotton, usually digitally or screen printed. It's the heart of Pakistani summer fashion.
  • Cotton and cambric: slightly heavier than lawn, great for everyday wear and office outfits.
  • Chiffon: sheer and flowing, used for dupattas and dressy shirts. It drapes beautifully for parties and weddings.
  • Khaddar: a thicker, textured hand-feel cotton that keeps you warm in fall and winter.
  • Karandi: a soft, slightly coarse winter weave with a matte finish, popular for cold-weather suits.
  • Silk and raw silk: rich and lustrous, favored for formal and bridal wear.

Embroidery and embellishment you should recognize

  • Chikankari: delicate white-on-white or tonal thread work, elegant for daytime events.
  • Zardozi: metallic thread embroidery, often with beads and sequins, seen on wedding wear.
  • Gota work: gold or silver ribbon appliqué on borders and necklines, a classic for mehndi.
  • Mirror work (shisha): small mirrors stitched into the fabric, adding sparkle and a folk-inspired look.
  • Tilla and dabka: fine metallic thread and coiled-wire work that gives formal pieces their raised, textured shine.

What are the main types of Pakistani outfits?

The main Pakistani outfits are the shalwar kameez, the kurta, the lawn three-piece suit, the sharara and gharara, the lehenga, the maxi or anarkali, and for men the kurta shalwar, often paired with a waistcoat.

  • Shalwar kameez: a tunic (kameez) worn with loose, gathered trousers (shalwar), usually with a dupatta. It's the everyday and festive staple for women and men.
  • Kurta: a tunic that can be worn with shalwar, straight trousers, cigarette pants or even jeans. Short kurtas are popular for casual and office looks.
  • Lawn three-piece suits: a printed lawn shirt, dyed trousers and a printed or chiffon dupatta. They're sold either unstitched (fabric pieces you take to a tailor) or stitched / ready-to-wear.
  • Sharara and gharara: wide, flared pants. A sharara flares from the waist; a gharara is fitted to the knee, then flares. Both are favorites for mehndi and wedding functions.
  • Lehenga: a full, flared skirt with a fitted choli or short shirt and a dupatta, typically for brides and close family at weddings.
  • Maxi and anarkali: floor-length, flared frocks that feel regal and photograph beautifully at formal events.
  • Men's kurta shalwar and waistcoat: the classic men's look for Jummah, Eid and weddings. A fitted waistcoat instantly makes a plain kurta formal.

Should you buy stitched or unstitched Pakistani suits in the USA?

Buy stitched (ready-to-wear) if you don't have easy access to a South Asian tailor or need an outfit quickly; buy unstitched if you have a trusted tailor and want a custom fit and design.

In Pakistan, unstitched lawn is the default because almost every neighborhood has a tailor. In the US, that's not always true. Tailors who understand kameez cuts, side slits and shalwar pleats are usually concentrated in larger metro areas, and custom stitching can take time, especially before Eid.

How do you choose the right Pakistani dress size in the US?

Measure your chest, waist, hips, shoulders and preferred length in inches, add a comfortable ease allowance, then compare with the size chart on the specific listing rather than your usual US size.

Pakistani dress sizes in the US are one of the biggest sources of returns and frustration. An "M" from one label can fit very differently from another, and none of them map neatly to US sizes like 6 or 8. Measure with a soft tape while wearing light clothing, keep the tape snug but not tight, and write the numbers down.

What is ease allowance and why it matters

Ease is the extra room between your body and the garment so you can move, sit and breathe comfortably. Traditional kameez cuts are not skin-tight, so many tailors add a few inches to the chest and hips for a relaxed fit, and less for a fitted look. If a listing gives garment measurements, compare them to your body measurements plus the ease you like. If you are between sizes, the bigger size is usually the safer choice, since taking a shirt in is easier than letting it out.

Always check each listing's size chart. Sizing varies by design and cut, so a chart on one product page may not apply to another. If anything is unclear, ask before you order and share your measurements so the team can help you choose.

How can you spot authentic Pakistani clothing vs a replica?

Authentic pieces usually show soft, breathable fabric, neat embroidery on the reverse side, crisp print detail, a dupatta that matches the quality of the shirt, and clean seams and labeling.

Replicas are common in online marketplaces. Some are honest, affordable copies sold as such; others are passed off as originals. You don't need to be an expert to tell the difference. Here's general guidance that works for almost any piece:

  1. Feel the fabric. Good lawn is fine, smooth and breathable, not stiff, plasticky or see-through in the wrong places. Khaddar should feel substantial and textured, not thin.
  2. Flip the embroidery. Look at the back. Quality work has dense, even stitches and tidy thread ends. Loose loops, gaps and heavy glue are warning signs.
  3. Zoom in on the print. Authentic digital prints have sharp edges and rich, consistent color. Blurry outlines, pixelation or color bleeding often point to a copy.
  4. Judge the dupatta. A replica often cuts corners here. Check the weight, the print quality and how the edges are finished.
  5. Check seams, labels and finishing. On stitched pieces, look for straight seams, neat hems and finished edges inside. Consistent labeling and packaging also help.
  6. Ask for real photos. A trustworthy seller is happy to share close-ups or answer questions about fabric and work before you buy.

When a price seems far too low for heavy hand embroidery or pure silk, take a moment to ask questions first. Real craftsmanship takes time and skill.

How do you care for Pakistani fabrics in US washers and dryers?

Wash most lawn and cotton suits inside out on a cold, gentle cycle with mild detergent, skip the hot dryer, and hand wash or dry clean anything embroidered, embellished, chiffon or silk.

  • Read the care label first and follow it over any general advice.
  • Wash dark and bright prints separately for the first few washes; some dyes can bleed.
  • Use cold water and the delicate cycle for lawn and cotton. Turn garments inside out to protect prints.
  • Avoid high dryer heat. It can shrink cotton and fade prints. Line dry or lay flat in shade, or use the lowest heat setting.
  • Hand wash or dry clean chiffon, silk, net and anything with zardozi, gota, sequins or mirror work. Never wring embellished pieces.
  • Iron on the reverse side at a medium setting, using a pressing cloth over embroidery.
  • Store formal wear folded in breathable cotton or muslin bags, not plastic, to protect metallic work.

What Pakistani clothes make good gifts?

Unstitched lawn or khaddar suits, classic kurtas and elegant dupattas make easy, size-flexible gifts, while men's kurta shalwar sets are a thoughtful choice for Eid and weddings.

For Eid

Eid outfits in the USA are often planned weeks ahead, so order early. For women, an embroidered lawn or cotton suit is a safe and welcome gift. For men, a crisp kurta shalwar, with or without a waistcoat, is a classic Eid morning look. If you're gifting to children, add a little extra room so it lasts through a growth spurt.

For weddings

For a bride's family or close relatives, a formal chiffon or silk suit fits the occasion. Because wedding wear is personal, an unstitched formal suit lets the recipient tailor it to her own taste.

For family visits

Visiting relatives in the US, or heading back home? Lightweight lawn suits pack small and suit most climates. Unstitched suits are especially practical gifts because you don't need to know the exact size.

Where can I buy Pakistani clothes in the USA?

You can buy Pakistani clothes in the USA from local South Asian boutiques or from online stores that specialize in Pakistani wear, such as Authentic Pak USA, where you can browse the collection and order through the site or WhatsApp.

What is the difference between stitched and unstitched Pakistani suits?

Unstitched suits come as fabric pieces that a tailor sews to your measurements, while stitched or ready-to-wear suits are already sewn in standard sizes and can be worn right away.

How do Pakistani dress sizes compare to US sizes?

Pakistani sizes do not map directly to US numeric sizes and vary by design, so measure your chest, waist, hips, shoulders and length in inches and compare them with the size chart on each listing.

What are Pakistani lawn suits?

Pakistani lawn suits are lightweight, breathable cotton outfits, usually a printed shirt, trousers and a dupatta, designed for warm weather and sold stitched or unstitched.

How can I tell if a Pakistani suit is authentic?

Check for soft, quality fabric, neat embroidery on the reverse side, sharp print detail, a dupatta of matching quality and clean seams, and ask the seller for close-up photos if you are unsure.

Can I machine wash Pakistani clothes?
